Offer

AI/ML model evaluation

You have two or more candidates and need to know which one to ship. They may be language models (LLMs), speech-to-text providers (ASR) or whole RAG setups, meaning systems that look things up in your documents before they answer. Latency is how long an answer takes.

In technical terms

Comparing LLMs, ASR, and RAG pipelines on real data: accuracy, latency, and cost, and a clear answer on which one holds up.

Who it is for
You need evaluations for an LLM product, including subjective ones that are quantitative and easy for end users to understand.
Questions it answers
Which model or vendor should we ship? Is our RAG chatbot actually right? Can we trust the eval numbers we were shown? Would a cheaper model do the same job?
What you get
A clear answer on which one holds up, with accuracy, latency and cost for each candidate. A detailed report with rankings, numbers and suggested next steps. The evaluation code and the test set. A walkthrough of the code and results on a call.
Related work
In my current role I benchmarked speech-to-text providers on accuracy, latency, and cost to guide production vendor decisions. The public experiments on this site are evaluations too. Read one as a case study
First step
A free 30-minute getting to know you and the problem call, followed by a scoping note within a week: the candidates, the test set, the metrics and KPIs and the price.

Offer

Hardening a private RAG system

A system that answers questions from your own documents and is used by your company alone. Retrieval is the search step that finds the passages an answer is built from. People see only what their role allows, and access is logged.

In technical terms

Single-tenant retrieval running inside a company's own AWS or Azure environment, with role-based access and audit logging from the start.

Who it is for
Anyone, frankly, but it suits teams whose data cannot leave their own servers.
Questions it answers
Can we use RAG on documents that cannot leave our own cloud? Who can see which documents, and is there a record of it?
What you get
Single-tenant retrieval running inside your own AWS or Azure environment, with role-based access and audit logging from the start. The code in your own repository, set up and running in your AWS or Azure account, with the infrastructure written as code so your team can rebuild it. Architecture notes and a runbook. A test set and script to keep checking answer quality. A handover session with your engineers.
Related work
Semantic search and RAG across legal, sales, and marketing repositories, and the customer-support system that cut ticket volume by up to 50%. The client results
First step
A free 30-minute getting to know you and the problem call, followed by a scoping note within a week: your documents and access rules, the architecture, how we’ll check answer quality, and the price.

A good first message for this offer

Our documents are [what kind] and have to stay inside [AWS or Azure]. [Who] needs to ask questions of them. Today we have [what exists]. The decision that hangs on this is [decision].

How I run my own experiments

In the experiments published on this site, what would count as a pass or a fail was written down before the results existed. Each write-up then lists where its findings stop holding, including the holes in my own plan.

For client work I do the same: we agree the pass/fail rule before anything runs, the report lists its limits, and a second model from a different family reviews the analysis.
