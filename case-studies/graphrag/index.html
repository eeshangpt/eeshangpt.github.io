Is a graph-based RAG system worth the extra work?

24 October 2026 · Eeshan Gupta

The question

I had said in public that graph-based RAG beats the plain vector kind, and I had never measured it. A graph system maps how the people, places and things in your documents relate to each other, at the cost of an LLM pass over every document and a graph to maintain.

Most comparisons use a plain baseline that a careful team would not ship, so I asked: if a graph system and a well-tuned standard one (keyword and meaning-based search combined, then reranked) hand the generator the same amount of text, how much of the graph's advantage is left?

What I tested

I wrote the decision rule down before the results existed: drop the "graph is overrated" angle only if the graph wins by more than 5 points on both datasets, with the lower end of its 95% interval above zero.

I used two public multi-hop question sets, MuSiQue and 2WikiMultiHopQA, with 950 questions each. Every system used the same generator, Claude Sonnet 5.5, and at most 1,000 tokens of context. Scores are token F1, a standard word-overlap score against the reference answer, out of 100.

In the pilot, a "give your best guess" line let the model answer many questions from memory, so the real run used a refusal prompt.

The answer

Here are the scores at a 1,000-token budget. The gap is worked out question by question.

Question setWell-tuned standardGraphGap, 95% interval
2WikiMultiHopQA37.256.2+19.0 [+16.1, +22.0]
MuSiQue34.838.2+3.5 [+0.4, +6.5]

On 2WikiMultiHopQA the graph won by 19.0 points. That is not close. On MuSiQue the gap was 3.5 points, and my equivalence test at plus or minus 4 points was inconclusive: it could not show the two systems are level, and it could not show the graph is clearly better.

MuSiQue failed my rule, so the verdict was iterate. Neither side of my original question is settled.

An exploratory split of 2WikiMultiHopQA by question type showed the graph ahead by 25 to 28 points on three of the four types, while the tuned hybrid was ahead by 6.5 points on plain comparison questions.

What it means for your team

The same two systems gave two different answers on two question sets, so test on your own questions before you pay for the graph. Compare against what you would really ship: against a plain dense baseline the graph scored higher on both sets, but against the tuned hybrid one of those wins shrank to a gap my test could not call.

GraphRAG is a good method when your data has clearly defined entity relationships, or at least supports them. That won't hold for every kind of data. Building the graph cost about $3.18 for MuSiQue and $4.47 for 2WikiMultiHopQA at a cheap reference API rate, and the cost of deploying it needs a thorough study of its own.

Limits

One model wrote every answer. Both question sets come from Wikipedia, and I would not extend these results to other documents. At 1,000 tokens the gold answer was in context for only 37 to 56% of MuSiQue questions (59 to 86% on 2WikiMultiHopQA), so every conclusion holds at that budget only.

I built the 2WikiMultiHopQA graph with Claude Sonnet, the same family as the generator, which could favour the graph there. The MuSiQue result is underpowered, at roughly two thirds power. I also missed two commitments: a second look at a 2,000-token budget, and a measurement of how often the generator fills gaps from memory. The article lists 8 limits in all.
