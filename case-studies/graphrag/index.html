How I check my own claims Case study, 24 October 2026 · Eeshan Gupta

Is a graph-based RAG system worth the extra work?

I had said in public that graph-based RAG beats the plain kind. Then I measured it. This page is the short version.

In short

I asked
Does a graph-based system beat the well-tuned standard one?
I found
Clearly on one set of questions. On the other, too thin to call.
For your team
Test on your own questions before you pay for the graph.

The question

GraphRAG first maps how the entities in your documents relate to each other. That costs an LLM pass over every document and a graph to maintain.

Most comparisons set it against a plain baseline that a careful team would not ship. So I compared it with the system I would deploy: keyword and meaning-based search combined, then re-sorted by a second model. I call that the well-tuned standard system. How much of the graph’s advantage is left when both get the same text?

What I found

I expected the graph to be a clear winner. On one set of test questions it won by 19.0 points. On the other the gap was 3.5, too thin for my own test to call either way.

What it means if you are choosing a system

Test on your own questions

Two sets of questions gave two different answers. Both are from Wikipedia, so I would not extend this to other kinds of documents.

Compare against what you would really ship

The graph beats a plain baseline on both sets. Against the well-tuned standard system, one win shrinks to a gap my test could not call.

Look at how your data is shaped

GraphRAG is a good method when your data has clearly defined entity relationships, or at least supports them. That won’t hold for every kind of data.

Study the cost before you commit

Building the graph cost about $3.18 and $4.47 at a cheap reference API rate. Deploying it needs a cost study of its own.

Where this stops holding

One model wrote every answer, and each system could pass on only a small, fixed amount of text, so every conclusion holds at that budget.

My rule, written before the run, returned “iterate”: neither side of my original question is settled.

Method, numbers and limits

Method and key numbers
Data
The questions come from MuSiQue and 2WikiMultiHopQA, in 1,000-question samples. The confirmatory run uses 950 questions per dataset, because the first 50 were the pilot.
Held equal
Every system used the same generator, Claude Sonnet 5.5, and at most 1,000 tokens of context, filled with whole passages in rank order.
The two systems
The tuned hybrid fuses BM25 keyword search and dense search with reciprocal rank fusion, then reranks with a cross-encoder (bge-reranker-base); its settings were fixed in advance. The graph system is HippoRAG 2-lite, which skips the query-time LLM step that filters triples.
Metric and uncertainty
I scored answers with token F1, the SQuAD word-overlap score. Uncertainty comes from a paired percentile bootstrap with 10,000 resamples and a 95% interval, and equivalence from TOST with a margin of ±4 points.
Rule written first
Drop the “graph is overrated” angle only if the graph wins by more than 5 points, with the 95% interval’s lower bound above zero, on both datasets.
Numbers
On 2Wiki the tuned hybrid scored 0.372 and the graph 0.562, a gap of +19.0 with a 95% CI of [+16.1, +22.0]. On MuSiQue they scored 0.348 and 0.382, a gap of +3.5 with a CI of [+0.4, +6.5]. The verdict was iterate.
Limits
The full write-up lists 8, including that I built the 2Wiki graph with Claude Sonnet, the same family as the generator, and that I missed two registered commitments.
