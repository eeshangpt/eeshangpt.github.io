Can a small local model judge RAG answers?

I tested whether a small model on my own PC could judge RAG answers. This page is the short version.

In short

I asked
Can a small model on my own PC judge whether an answer sticks to its source?
I found
It ranked generators mostly in line with human annotators, then failed on fresh data.
For your team
Confirm a judge on a second dataset before you rely on it.

The question

A judge here is a model that checks another model’s answers. Mine checks whether a RAG answer sticks to its source (faithfulness), the job a large paid model usually does. My machine has a 6 GB graphics card, and judging is costly, so I tried a small local model.

I tried two ideas on public data, with the rules committed in git before I computed any result. Everything ran locally.

What I found

The first idea, ranking generators by how often each hallucinates, passed its pilot and failed on fresh data. The second, a layered judge, came closer and still missed its target on both datasets.

What it means if you are choosing a system

Confirm on fresh data

The pilot ranked generators mostly in line with human annotators. On a second dataset, with the setup unchanged, agreement fell to 0.048.

A small model can be a gate

A cascade, or a small model tuned for the task, can gate a larger judge, so the larger model only sees the difficult cases.

Know what a miss costs you

Until I retest, I would use a small model only where it rarely passes an unfaithful answer as faithful, and where wrongly flagging a good answer costs little.

Where this stops holding

FaithBench items were chosen where five existing detectors disagreed most, so the data is hard by construction. With 80 summaries per generator, the test could resolve the coarse split and not the order inside a tier.

The layered judge’s target comes from a preprint’s samples, which differ from mine, and I picked the primary model after seeing earlier results. Treat it as a lead worth confirming.

Method and key numbers
Judges
I used Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 in 4-bit as a yes/no judge, and MiniCheck-Flan-T5-Large, a small fact-checking model. The rules were committed and tagged before any result.
Ranking idea, data and rule
The pilot used the six generators in the RAGTruth QA test split, 150 items each. The confirmation used FaithBench: 800 summaries from 10 LLMs over the same 80 documents. Scale needed Kendall tau-b of at least 0.5 and the right tier order, and kill meant a wrong tier order or tau-b below 0.3.
Ranking idea, numbers
In the pilot, tau-b was 0.733 (95% bootstrap interval [0.414, 1.000]) with the tier order correct, so the verdict was scale. On FaithBench it was 0.048 ([-0.167, 0.523]) with the tier order wrong, so the verdict was kill. The judge flagged 9% to 20% of summaries per generator against 46% to 73% from annotators.
Layered judge
MiniCheck answers first and unsure items go to Qwen3-4B. The target was Cohen’s kappa of 0.9 times the Claude Sonnet kappa reported in the RAIM preprint: 0.493 on WiCE (172 items) and 0.541 on RAGTruth (300 items). The results were 0.488 and 0.313, and the verdict was iterate.
Limits
The full write-up lists every limit, including that the 80 source documents are shared by all 10 generators and that the 0.5 cut was not calibrated.
